### Cross-policy comparison (moved out of the paper — supporting-doc only)
The paper reports only the adopted policy (**SRRIP-Live**). The comparison of the
three liveness insertion policies — each vs its **own** non-guided base, current
config, mode-5, θ=6 — lives here. Figure: `results/plots/live_vs_base.png`.

| suite | LRU-Live/LRU | SRRIP-Live/SRRIP | IPV-Live/IPV |
|-------|-------------|------------------|--------------|
| dpc3  | **+4.80%**  | +2.25%  | −0.55% |
| gap   | +3.82%      | +2.05%  | +6.26% |
| gms   | +0.38%      | +1.28%  | −1.29% |
| ligra | **+2.95%**  | +1.53%  | −3.02% |
| ml    | +2.37%      | **+8.80%** | **−9.77%** |
| qmm   | +1.40%      | +0.99%  | +1.75% |

(all complete; ligra at 17/18 traces.)

**Finding: LRU-Live and SRRIP-Live are both robust** (positive on every suite);
**IPV-Live is fragile** (positive only on gap/qmm; strongly negative on
dpc3/gms/ligra/ml) — its high-RRPV insertion punishes mispredicted-dead blocks hard
(verified fair: `ipv_base`=0% / `ipv_live`=100% activation, identical config
otherwise). **LRU-Live and SRRIP-Live trade wins** — LRU-Live beats SRRIP-Live on
dpc3 (+4.80 vs +2.25) and ligra (+2.95 vs +1.53); SRRIP-Live wins big on ML
(+8.80 vs +2.37). Neither dominates, so the paper adopts **SRRIP-Live for hardware
cost** (2-bit RRPV vs ⌈log₂N⌉ LRU position) at competitive performance — not because
it always wins. Data: `lru_live_vs_base_*`, `ipv_live_vs_base_*`,
`bypass_allsuites .../repl_only`.
*(IPV-Live old MID-scoring vs mode-5 is a wash vs IPV; see conversation 2026-06-15.)*

`ours_sizestudy` is the broken bounded-path version with ~zero bypasses).

## 2. History-table internals

- **Internal replacement policy: LFU > SRRIP > LRU** for the history table itself
  (exp003). **TIERED** beats LRU only with active ROI churn + liveness diversity;
  degenerate at cap=256 (see [[project_repl_policy_finding]]). *(QMM, current.)*
- **Bounded tables: effective capacity = main table + promotion buffer**; cap=0
  means unbounded, not empty.
- **Size study (running): per-PC needs very little storage.** `ours_sizestudy_20260614_1813`
  sweeps `REPL_HIST_LLC_CAP ∈ {0,4096,2048,1024,512,256}` (floor 256). Expectation
  from measured occupancy: QMM (max ~35K) drops first, DPC3 (~5K) next,
  GAP/GMS/Ligra/ML (≤2K, mostly ≤600) hold at 256. *Knee TBD.*

## 3. Scoring / activation mechanism

- **Mode-5 (3-class DoA/MD/MA, state-dependent δ=5/3/1, θ_doa=6/θ_dead=10) chosen.**
  Data: `*_scoring_modes_20260608`, `*_mode5_20260610`.
- **Counting mode is a minor knob for the bypass decision.** The bypass test reads
  *only* the doa counter (`classify(key, thd, 0)`, thd_dead=0); the scoring mode
  only changes the doa step size. This is why mode differences are small for bypass.
- **Activation = DoA via accessed-bit + halflife; recheck/backoff breaks lock-in**
  (modest, workload-dependent): backoff −0.20% ALL, helps Ligra +1.27%, costs QMM.

## 4. Bypass predictor (current config, vs SRRIP)

- **LLC per-PC bypass: +9.78% ALL** combined (bypass+repl), **+9.34%** bypass-only
  (SimPoint-weighted: DPC3/SPEC slices weighted, other suites equal per-trace weight).
  Per suite (combined): DPC3 +17.3%, Ligra +34.9%, ML +11.7%, GAP +4.9%, QMM +1.6%,
  GMS +1.6%. (Unweighted ALL was +12.17%; DPC3 was +22.6% — over-counted multi-slice
  SPEC benchmarks.) Data: `bypass_allsuites_20260610_2312`,
  `bypass_only_allsuites_20260611_1200`.
- **Per-PC is the multiplier: +11.51% (PC) vs +3.17% (address)**, bypass-only,
  everything else identical. Data: `addr_indexed_20260611_1806`. See EVIDENCE §3–4.
- **Bypass × replacement-guidance interaction is small** (repl adds ~0.6 pp on PC,
  and *hurts* the address variant).

## 5. Victim cache (bypass shadow) — neutral

- **Adding a victim cache for bypassed blocks does essentially nothing**
  (+13.6–13.8% ALL with shadow vs +13.56% without; no size trend; Ligra +35.5–36.7
  vs +35.4). Data: `shadow_allsuites_20260613_0928`. Interpretation: on the suites
  where we bypass heavily the predictor is accurate, so bypassed blocks are truly
  dead and the shadow sees few useful hits.

## 6. Set-sampling — near-free and *more robust*

- **~4 sampler sets recover the full benefit** (+13.97% vs +13.56% full); more
  sampler sets only lose benefit by holding sets out of the policy.
  Data: `sample_pf_allsuites_20260613_2330`.
- **It also prevents over-bypass collapse** (full self-training craters PageRank
  −42%, Triangle −19% via a feedback loop; the policy-free sampler never enters it).
  So sampling is cheaper hardware *and* more stable — the Hawkeye/SDBP rationale.

## 7. Cross-level STLB→LLC propagation — dead end

- **No propagation variant beats no-propagation (+13.07% ALL).** PC-gate is
  actively harmful (−8.5 pp); PFN gates are mildly negative (~−1 pp).
  Data: `propgate_allsuites_20260614_0147`. The LLC predictor already captures the
  signal; feeding it the STLB PC only adds noise.

## 8. Comparison with Mazumdar — where our advantage comes from

- **Ours (LLC-only) +12.17% vs Mazumdar full LLC+STLB +0.66%** (+0.07% at paper
  threshold). Ours wins *without even bypassing in the STLB*.
- **The advantage = 3-class per-PC liveness scoring (enabler) × per-PC aggregation
  (multiplier).** Ruled out: threshold, hashing, PFQ gating per se (EVIDENCE §3,5).
- **Budget: per-PC needs ~100–350 entries** (5/6 suites fit in <512) **vs per-address
  ~1.6M**; same per-entry bits, ours uses the budget better. Faithful (hashed,
  bounded) Maz and our raw-bounded Maz both fire ~0 bypasses.
- **Morph convergence proven:** the access-PC cbPred (R4) reproduces ours (R5) to
  0.01% IPC. Full ladder + proof in [`EVIDENCE.md`](EVIDENCE.md) §7.
  Data: `maz_morph_20260614_1813` (running), `maz_comparison`/`maz_equiv`/`cbpred_*`.
